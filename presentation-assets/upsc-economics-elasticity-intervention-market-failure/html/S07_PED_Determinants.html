Substitutes
More elastic when
close substitutes exist
For example
One brand of tea, not tea in general
Necessity
More elastic when
the good is discretionary
For example
Restaurant meals, not cooking gas
Budget share
More elastic when
it takes a large share of income
For example
A two-wheeler, not a box of matches
Time
More elastic when
buyers have longer to adjust
For example
Petrol over several years, not this week
Market definition
More elastic when
the market is narrowly defined
For example
One rice brand, not food as a whole
These are tendencies, not rules: the same good can be more elastic for some households, places and periods than for others.
