T
Time period
More time turns unavailable substitutes into available ones: habits change, equipment is replaced.
Longer period → more price elastic
I
Income proportion
A large share of the budget forces buyers to look for alternatives; a trivial purchase does not.
Larger share → more price elastic
N
Necessity
If the buyer cannot do without it, quantity demanded barely falls when the price rises.
More necessary → more price inelastic
S
Substitutes
Can buyers switch?
Number and closeness of alternatives, minus the cost of switching (contracts, loyalty schemes, ecosystems)
More and closer substitutes → more price elastic
Determinant→ability to switch, delay or do without→size of % response in QD→|PED|
